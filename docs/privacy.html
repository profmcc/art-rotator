Privacy Policy

Ars Serena for iPhone, Android and Mac · Last updated 15 September 2026

Ars Serena collects nothing about you. There is no account, no analytics, no advertising, no tracking, and no server belonging to this app, on any of the three platforms. Everything it creates stays on your own device, and removing the app removes it. The rest of this page explains that in the detail that privacy law expects, platform by platform where they differ.

Who is responsible

Ars Serena is published by an independent developer. For any question about this policy or about your data, write to profmccarthy@protonmail.com. Because no personal data is collected or processed, no Data Protection Officer is appointed; the contact above is the responsible person for all privacy enquiries and will answer them directly.

Which apps this covers

  • Ars Serena for iPhone, distributed through the App Store.
  • Ars Serena for Android, distributed through Google Play (package io.github.profmcc.arsserena).
  • Ars Serena for Mac, a menu-bar app distributed as a direct download.

What the apps collect

No personal data whatsoever. None of the apps has a sign-up, a login, a profile, a contact form, telemetry or a crash-reporting SDK. They do not read your contacts, location, calendar, health data, microphone or camera. They do not use an advertising identifier, and they perform no tracking — so the iPhone app never shows Apple's tracking prompt, because it has nothing to ask for.

iPhone

Stored inside the app's private container, which its widgets share. If you turn on Mirror to Photos album, finished wallpapers are also added to an album named “Ars Serena” in your photo library, so that iOS can rotate your Lock Screen through it; iOS provides no way for an app to set the wallpaper directly. That album remains in Photos if you delete the app, and you can delete it yourself at any time. Like any app's data, the container is included in your iPhone's own iCloud or computer backup if you use one; the developer has no access to those backups.

Android

Stored in the app's private storage, which other apps cannot read. If you choose a lock-screen picture from your photos, the system photo picker hands the app that one picture, and a copy is kept in the same private storage.

If Android backup is turned on for your Google account, Android includes the app's settings, the list of artworks already shown, your saved shelves and the lock-screen picture you chose, so they return on a new phone. That backup is made and held by Google under Google's terms, and the developer has no access to it. The download queue and the short history of recent artworks are deliberately left out of it.

Mac

Stored in a folder in your own Pictures folder, ~/Pictures/ArtWallpapers, which you can open and inspect at any time. Settings are small files in the same folder, and it also holds a local log of which artworks were shown and a local diagnostics log. The saved-artworks gallery is an ordinary web page in that folder; it opens in your browser from the disk and loads nothing from the internet. If you choose to add free API keys for extra museums, they are kept only in ~/.config/art-rotator/keys.json, a file you create yourself.

Permissions

iPhone — photo library access, optional and off by default

The iPhone app requests no permissions at all unless you ask it to. Photo library access is the only permission it can ever use. It is off when you install the app, and it is requested at the moment you turn on Mirror to Photos album in Settings — not on launch, and not in the background.

It exists for one purpose: to create and manage the app's own “Ars Serena” album, adding finished wallpapers and removing ones that have rotated out, so that the system's Photo Shuffle has something to rotate through. It never reads, opens, uploads or analyses any other photo in your library. Declining, or never enabling it, leaves the app fully functional: the Shortcuts method described in the app's setup guide puts artwork on your Lock Screen without any photo library access.

One other switch brings a question from iOS. Turning on Show on the Dynamic Island in Settings makes iOS ask, the first time the artwork reaches your Lock Screen, whether to allow Live Activities from Ars Serena. A Live Activity shows the current artwork and its caption on the Lock Screen and in the Dynamic Island; it gives the app no access to anything of yours. It is off when you install the app.

Android — four permissions, none of which ever shows a prompt

All four are what Android calls normal permissions: granted at install, never asked about, and listed on the Play Store page.

Mac — no permission prompts

The Mac app asks for no permissions. It sets the desktop picture through macOS's own interface for doing so, which needs none. To change the artwork on schedule when its menu is closed, it registers a login item and a background agent, both visible and switchable in System Settings → General → Login Items & Extensions.

Network activity and third parties

The apps make outbound requests to exactly one kind of destination: the public open-access services of museums and archives, over HTTPS, to download public-domain and openly licensed artwork and its descriptive metadata.

These requests are downloads only. They carry the app's name with a link to its support page, which Wikimedia and some museums ask for so they can reach the developer, and search terms chosen by the app. They carry no account, no identifier, no device fingerprint and nothing about you. An API key you add on the Mac is sent only to the institution that issued it, because its service requires it.

One unavoidable technical disclosure, stated plainly: as with loading any web page, those servers necessarily see the IP address a request comes from, and each institution's own privacy policy governs what it does with its server logs. The apps have no influence over that and receive nothing back except artwork and metadata.

When you tap or click to open an artwork's page, it opens in your web browser, and from then on you are visiting that museum's website under its own policy.

There are no analytics providers, no advertising networks, no social SDKs, no payment processors and no third-party software development kits of any kind in any of the apps.

Artificial intelligence and image analysis

The apps send nothing to any external AI or machine-learning service. No prompts, no images and no text leave the device for processing. Where an app examines an artwork — to decide where to crop it, or to recognise a photograph of a framed object — that happens on the device, using the operating system's own frameworks.

Legal basis for processing

Under the EU/UK GDPR, Thailand's Personal Data Protection Act B.E. 2562 (PDPA) and comparable frameworks, a legal basis is required to process personal data. These apps process no personal data, so no legal basis is engaged. The only processing that occurs is the technically necessary transmission of a network request to a museum's server, which is performed when the app fetches artwork, and is necessary to provide the function you installed it for.

Your rights

Privacy law gives you rights to access, correct, delete, restrict, object to, and port your personal data, and to withdraw consent as easily as you gave it. Those rights are fully satisfied here, and you can exercise them yourself without asking anyone:

  • Access — everything the apps hold is visible in the app, in your Photos album on iPhone, and as ordinary files in ~/Pictures/ArtWallpapers on the Mac. There is no hidden profile, because there is no profile.
  • Deletion — on iPhone, deleting the app removes its data; the “Ars Serena” album can be deleted from Photos. On Android, uninstall the app, or clear its storage in Settings → Apps → Ars Serena → Storage. On the Mac, quit the app and move it to the Bin, which removes its login item, then delete the ArtWallpapers folder from Pictures. The wallpaper you last had stays until you change it.
  • Withdrawal of consent — on iPhone, revoke photo library access at any time in Settings → Privacy & Security → Photos → Ars Serena. The app continues to work; only the album stops updating.
  • Restriction and objection — pausing rotation, turning off automatic downloads where the app offers it, or removing the app stops its network activity.
  • Portability — saved artworks are ordinary JPEG files.

There is no data on our side to request, correct or erase, because none is ever sent to us. If you would like that confirmed in writing, write to the contact address above and it will be confirmed.

Children

The apps are not directed at children, collect no data from anyone regardless of age, and have no social features, messaging, user accounts or user-generated content. They therefore do not knowingly collect personal information from children under 13 (COPPA) or under 16 (GDPR), because they do not collect personal information from anyone.

Please note that the apps display works from historical museum collections, which include classical and artistic nudity, religious imagery and depictions of violence. The App Store and Google Play age ratings reflect this.

Data security

All network requests use HTTPS. No credentials, tokens or personal data exist in the apps, apart from any API key you add to the Mac app yourself, so there is nothing to encrypt at rest beyond what your operating system already protects, and nothing that could be exposed by a breach of a server — because there is no server.

Data transfers

No personal data is transferred anywhere, internationally or otherwise. The services listed above are operated in the United States, Denmark, Japan, the Netherlands and the European Union, and by the Wikimedia Foundation and GitHub; contacting them is a download, not a transfer of your data.

Apple and Google

If you have opted in to sharing analytics with Apple, Apple may provide the developer with aggregate, anonymised statistics such as install counts and crash reports. Google Play likewise gives developers aggregate statistics, such as install counts and crash and ANR reports, gathered by Android and Google Play rather than by the app. These are services of the stores, not of Ars Serena; they identify no individual to the developer, and they are governed by Apple's Privacy Policy and Google's Privacy Policy.
