Ars Serena puts public-domain artwork from museum open-access collections on your wallpaper — The Met, the Art Institute of Chicago, the Cleveland Museum of Art, SMK in Copenhagen, Wikimedia Commons and more — cropped to your screen and captioned with the title, artist and date. It never shows the same artwork twice.

iPhone

First-time setup

iOS does not allow any app to change your wallpaper. Apple provides no way to do it. So the app prepares finished wallpapers and iOS puts them up. Pick whichever of these suits you; the app's own Set Up screen shows the same two.

Option A — Shortcuts (any interval, and asks for nothing)

Shortcuts hands the picture straight to the system, so the app never touches your photo library and never asks to.

  1. Open Shortcuts → Automation → +.
  2. Choose Time of Day, pick a start time, set it to repeat hourly, and choose Run Immediately.
  3. Add the action Get Next Artwork Image (it appears under Ars Serena).
  4. Add the action Set Wallpaper, give it that image, and pick Lock Screen, Home Screen, or both.
  5. For every 30 minutes, add a second automation starting half an hour after the first.

To keep one artwork on the Lock Screen while the Home Screen rotates: in the Gallery, press and hold an artwork and choose Use for Lock Screen, then use the Get Lock Screen Artwork Image action with Set Wallpaper set to Lock Screen, and point the rotating automation at Home Screen only.

Option B — Photo Shuffle (changes hourly, needs Photos access)

  1. In Ars Serena, open Settings and turn on Mirror to Photos album. iOS asks for photo access at that moment. Nothing asks you for anything before this point.
  2. Tap Fetch a batch now and wait about a minute.
  3. Press and hold your Lock Screen, tap Customize, then +.
  4. Choose Photo Shuffle → Album → Ars Serena, set the frequency to hourly, and add it.

Photo access is used only to keep that one album: the app adds finished wallpapers and tidies out old ones. It never reads any other photo in your library. If you decline, the switch goes back off and Option A still works.

My wallpaper isn't changing

Check that Settings shows artworks ready under Cache; if it says 0, tap Fetch a batch now. With Shortcuts, open Shortcuts → Automation and check the automation is on and set to run immediately. With Photo Shuffle, check the Lock Screen is pointed at the Ars Serena album rather than a single photo.

The cache isn't filling up on its own

By default batches download only while the iPhone is charging and on Wi-Fi, so the app never spends your mobile data or battery, and iOS decides when background work actually runs. Fetch a batch now fills it at once, and both conditions can be turned off in Settings.

I don't see the Dynamic Island controls

They are off until you turn them on: in the app, open Settings and switch on Show on the Dynamic Island. The first time the artwork reaches your Lock Screen, iOS asks whether to allow Live Activities from Ars Serena.

You need an iPhone with a Dynamic Island, and the app must be in the background. Long-press the island to expand it for thumbs up and down, save, zoom, the museum's page, back and skip. If it never appears, check Settings → Ars Serena → Live Activities. iOS ends a Live Activity after about eight hours; opening the app brings it back.

How much storage does it use? Does it drain the battery?

Roughly 1 MB per cached artwork; the Keep … on hand setting sets how many. Between batches iOS suspends the app completely, so there is nothing running in the background.

Can I use it on iPad?

No. The iPhone app renders its wallpapers at iPhone proportions.

Android

Getting started

There is nothing to set up. Android lets an app set the wallpaper directly, so Ars Serena just does it. Open the app once; it downloads a few artworks and starts changing your wallpaper on its own, and keeps doing so after the phone restarts.

In Settings you can choose how often it changes, whether downloads wait for Wi-Fi or pause on low battery, and Fetch a batch now. Turn on Home screen only to keep your lock screen out of it; the lock screen can then keep what it shows, go back to the picture the phone came with, or show one you choose.

What it asks for

Nothing you have to answer. Google Play lists four permissions, all granted at install with no dialog: full network access (to download artwork), set wallpaper, view network connections (so downloads can wait for Wi-Fi), and run at startup (so the schedule survives a restart). Choosing a lock-screen picture uses the system photo picker, which gives the app only the one picture you pick.

My wallpaper isn't changing

A scheduled change waits until the screen is off, so the phone never recolours under your thumb. Open the app and tap Next to change it now. Otherwise check that Change automatically is on and that Settings shows artworks ready; if not, tap Fetch a batch now. Android runs background work no more often than every 15 minutes, and a battery saver can delay it longer.

It changes my lock screen too

Turn on Home screen only in Settings and choose what the lock screen shows. You can also pick a lock-screen picture from the Gallery.

How do I remove everything?

Uninstall the app, or clear its storage in Settings → Apps → Ars Serena → Storage. The wallpaper you last had stays until you change it.

Mac

Getting started

  1. Open the disk image and drag Ars Serena to Applications.
  2. Open it from Applications. If macOS says it cannot verify the developer, Control-click the app, choose Open, and confirm. You only do this once.
  3. A picture-frame icon appears in the menu bar. That is the whole setup.

The app asks for no permissions. It appears in System Settings → General → Login Items & Extensions, which is what lets it change the picture while you work; you can switch it off there.

What's in the menu?

Next and Previous; Save to Gallery, where saving twice makes a favourite; Hold This Artwork for a while or until you resume; Change Every; Zoom for the artwork on screen; Fit Whole Artworks by Default for black bars instead of cropping; and Open Gallery, a page of everything you have kept, where any artwork can go back on your desktop.

My desktop isn't changing

Check the menu isn't holding an artwork, and that Ars Serena is switched on in Login Items & Extensions. Downloads can pause on a metered connection or under 20% battery (see Downloads in the menu), but the picture keeps changing from what is already on your Mac.

Where are my artworks, and how do I remove everything?

Everything lives in ~/Pictures/ArtWallpapers. To remove the app, quit it from the menu and move it to the Bin, which removes its login item too, then delete that folder if you want the artworks gone.

All apps

I don't want to see a particular artwork

No artwork is ever shown twice, so moving on with Next (or skip) is enough. On iPhone, thumbs down in the Dynamic Island also skips and shows that artist less often. Historical collections include classical nudity, religious imagery and depictions of violence.

What does “Only artworks shaped like the screen” do?

It keeps to artworks within about 15% of your screen's proportions, so there is little to crop or to fill with bars. It is experimental: most artworks are another shape, so the app goes through many more of them to find these — more museum requests, more data, and new artworks arrive slowly. Where a museum publishes an image's size, the wrong shapes are skipped before downloading.

What does “Include attribution-required art” do?

Off, the default, the apps use only works free worldwide with no conditions (CC0 and Public Domain Mark). On, they also accept works free to reuse with credit, and print that credit on the wallpaper. Anything whose rights cannot be positively identified is never downloaded.

Where does the art come from, and is it legal to use?

From museum and archive open-access programmes, and every item's rights statement is checked before it is downloaded. The collections and licences are listed in the apps under Sources (on iPhone, Credits & Licensing). Requests go only to those services, carry nothing about you, and name the app with a link to this page, so a museum can reach the developer.

What does the app know about me?

Nothing. No account, no analytics, no advertising, no tracking, and nothing is ever uploaded. The details are in the privacy policy.

Reporting a bug

Each app can put a report together for you: Settings → Report a problem on iPhone and Android, and Report a Problem… in the Mac's menu. It opens a draft in your mail app with the app's version, your device and the app's recent log; add what happened, then send it. Nothing is sent until you do.

Or email profmccarthy@protonmail.com with:

  • your device and its system version (for example iPhone 16 with iOS 26, Pixel 9 with Android 16, or a MacBook Air with macOS 26);
  • what you expected to happen, and what happened instead;
  • a screenshot if it is something visible;
  • the title of the artwork involved, if it is about a specific image.

The apps have no analytics, so nothing is reported automatically — if you don't tell me, I won't know.
