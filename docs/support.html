What the app does

Ars Serena downloads public-domain artwork from museum open-access collections — The Met, the Art Institute of Chicago, the Cleveland Museum of Art, SMK in Copenhagen, Japan Search, and Wikimedia Commons — crops each piece to your screen, prints the title, artist and year onto it in a frosted-glass caption, and keeps a stack of them ready so your Lock Screen can rotate through real art instead of a stock photo.

First-time setup

The one thing to understand up front: iOS does not allow any app to change your wallpaper. Apple provides no API for it. So the app prepares finished wallpapers into a photo album, and iOS rotates them for you. Pick whichever of these suits you.

Option A — Photo Shuffle (easiest, changes hourly)

  1. Open Ars Serena and tap Fetch a batch now. Allow photo access when asked. Wait about a minute.
  2. Press and hold your Lock Screen, tap Customize, then + to add a wallpaper.
  3. Choose Photo Shuffle → Album → Art Rotator.
  4. Set Shuffle Frequency to Hourly, then Add → Set as Wallpaper Pair.

Option B — Shortcuts (any interval you like, including 30 minutes)

  1. Open Shortcuts → Automation → +.
  2. Choose Time of Day, pick a start time, set Repeat: Hourly, then Next.
  3. Choose New Blank Automation.
  4. Add the action Get Next Artwork Image (it appears under Ars Serena).
  5. Add the action Set Wallpaper. Tap its image field and choose the output of the previous step. Pick Lock Screen, Home Screen, or both.
  6. Turn off “Ask Before Running”, then Done.
  7. For every 30 minutes, make a second identical automation starting 30 minutes after the first — iOS only repeats hourly, so two staggered automations give you a half-hour cadence.

Common questions

My wallpaper isn't changing

Almost always one of three things. Check the app shows artworks ready in the Cache section — if it says 0, tap Fetch a batch now. Then confirm your Lock Screen is actually set to Photo Shuffle pointed at the Ars Serena album, not to a static image. If you used the Shortcuts route, open Shortcuts → Automation and check the automation is enabled and “Ask Before Running” is off.

The cache isn't filling up on its own

By design, batches only download while your iPhone is charging and on Wi-Fi, so the app never spends your mobile data or battery. Plug in on Wi-Fi and leave it for a while. iOS also decides when background tasks actually run, so it can take a few hours to top up on its own. You can always tap Fetch a batch now to fill it immediately. Both conditions can be turned off in the app if you would rather it fetch any time.

I don't see the Dynamic Island controls

Three requirements: an iPhone 14 Pro or newer (older iPhones have no Dynamic Island), the app must have been opened at least once, and the app must be in the background — while it is on screen, iOS shows a plain pill instead. Long-press the island to expand it and reveal thumbs up/down, save, zoom, website and skip. If it never appears, check Settings → Ars Serena → Live Activities is on.

iOS also ends Live Activities automatically after about eight hours. Opening the app brings it back.

An artwork is cropped oddly, or I want to see the whole thing

Tap the zoom button in the expanded Dynamic Island — the two diagonal arrows. That switches the current artwork between filling the screen and showing the entire work with borders. The app already turns very long paintings a quarter turn automatically so that panoramas and scrolls survive a portrait screen, but you can always override it.

I don't want to see a particular artwork

Tap Hide on the main screen. That artwork is removed immediately and will never be downloaded again. Historical collections include classical nudity, religious imagery and depictions of violence, and Hide is there so you decide what belongs on your own Lock Screen.

What does “Include attribution-required art” do?

Off (the default), the app only uses works that are free worldwide with no conditions — CC0 and Public Domain Mark. On, it also accepts works that are free to reuse with credit, and prints that credit onto the wallpaper beneath the title. It noticeably increases how much Japanese material you see, since much of Japan Search is licensed that way. Anything whose rights cannot be positively identified is never downloaded under either setting.

How much storage and data does it use?

Roughly 1 MB per cached artwork, so the 50–150 slider works out to about 50–150 MB. Downloads happen in batches while charging on Wi-Fi. The app requests a screen-sized version of each image rather than the museum's full-resolution master, which is typically eight to twenty times smaller.

Does it drain my battery?

It shouldn't. Between batches iOS suspends the app completely — no background process, no polling, no timers. Work happens in short bursts while you are plugged in.

Where does the art come from, and is it legal to use?

All of it comes from museum and archive open-access programmes, and every item's rights statement is checked before it is downloaded. Anything that cannot be positively identified as freely reusable is skipped. The full list of collections and licences is in the app under Credits & Licensing.

How do I remove everything?

Delete the app — that removes its entire data store. The “Ars Serena” album stays in Photos so you don't lose anything unexpectedly; delete it from the Photos app if you want it gone. Remember to switch your Lock Screen back to another wallpaper first.

Can I use it on iPad?

No. The app is iPhone-only. Its wallpapers are rendered at portrait iPhone dimensions and its controls live in the Dynamic Island, neither of which exists on iPad.

Reporting a bug

Email profmccarthy@protonmail.com with:

  • your iPhone model and iOS version;
  • what you expected to happen, and what happened instead;
  • a screenshot if it is something visible;
  • the title of the artwork involved, if it is about a specific image.

The app has no analytics, so nothing is reported automatically — if you don't tell me, I won't know.
