Privacy Policy

Ars Serena for iOS · Last updated 23 August 2026

Ars Serena collects nothing about you. There is no account, no analytics, no advertising, no tracking, and no server belonging to this app. Everything it creates stays on your iPhone, and deleting the app deletes all of it. The rest of this page explains that in the detail that privacy law expects.

Who is responsible

Ars Serena is published by an independent developer. For any question about this policy or about your data, write to profmccarthy@protonmail.com. Because no personal data is collected or processed, no Data Protection Officer is appointed; the contact above is the responsible person for all privacy enquiries and will answer them directly.

What the app collects

No personal data whatsoever. The app has no sign-up, no login, no profile, no contact form, no telemetry and no crash-reporting SDK. It does not read your photos, contacts, location, calendar, health data, microphone or camera. It does not use the advertising identifier, and it performs no tracking as defined by Apple's App Tracking Transparency framework — so it never shows a tracking prompt, because it has nothing to ask for.

Photo library access — optional, and off by default

The app requests no permissions at all unless you ask it to. Photo library access is the only permission it can ever use, it is off when you install the app, and it is requested at the moment you turn on Mirror to Photos album in Settings — not on launch, and not in the background.

It exists for one purpose: to create and manage the app’s own “Ars Serena” album, adding finished wallpapers and removing ones that have rotated out, so that the system’s Photo Shuffle has something to rotate through. It never reads, opens, uploads or analyses any other photo in your library.

Declining, or never enabling it, leaves the app fully functional: the Shortcuts method described in the app’s setup guide puts artwork on your Lock Screen without any photo library access whatsoever.

Network activity and third parties

The app makes outbound requests to exactly one category of destination: the public open-access APIs of museums and archives, over HTTPS, to download public-domain artwork and its descriptive metadata. These are:

  • The Metropolitan Museum of Art (New York)
  • Art Institute of Chicago
  • Cleveland Museum of Art
  • SMK — National Gallery of Denmark
  • Japan Search, operated by the National Diet Library (Japan)
  • Wikimedia Commons

These requests are downloads only. They carry a generic application name and the search terms used to find artwork. They carry no account, no identifier, no device fingerprint and nothing about you.

One unavoidable technical disclosure, stated plainly: as with loading any web page, those servers necessarily see the IP address the request comes from, and each institution's own privacy policy governs what it does with its server logs. This app has no influence over that and receives nothing back from them except artwork and metadata.

There are no analytics providers, no advertising networks, no social SDKs, no payment processors and no third-party software development kits of any kind embedded in this app.

Artificial intelligence services

The app does not send anything to any external AI or machine-learning service. No prompts, no images and no text leave the device for processing.

Legal basis for processing

Under the EU/UK GDPR, Thailand's Personal Data Protection Act B.E. 2562 (PDPA) and comparable frameworks, a legal basis is required to process personal data. This app processes no personal data, so no legal basis is engaged. The only processing that occurs is the technically necessary transmission of a network request to a museum's server, which is performed at your instruction when the app fetches artwork, and is necessary to provide the function you asked for.

Your rights

Privacy law gives you rights to access, correct, delete, restrict, object to, and port your personal data, and to withdraw consent as easily as you gave it. Those rights are fully satisfied here, and you can exercise them yourself without asking anyone:

  • Access — everything the app holds is visible in the app and in your Photos album. There is no hidden profile, because there is no profile.
  • Deletion — deleting the app removes its entire data container. The “Ars Serena” photo album can be deleted from the Photos app. Individual artworks can be removed at any time with Hide.
  • Withdrawal of consent — revoke photo library access at any time in Settings → Privacy & Security → Photos → Ars Serena. The app continues to work; only the album stops updating.
  • Restriction and objection — turning off automatic batches, or simply not opening the app, stops all network activity entirely.
  • Portability — saved artworks are ordinary JPEG files and can be exported from Photos like any other image.

There is no data on our side to request, correct or erase, because none is ever sent to us. If you would like that confirmed in writing, write to the contact address above and it will be confirmed.

Children

The app is not directed at children, collects no data from anyone regardless of age, and has no social features, messaging, user accounts or user-generated content. It therefore does not knowingly collect personal information from children under 13 (COPPA) or under 16 (GDPR), because it does not collect personal information from anyone.

Please note the app displays works from historical museum collections, which include classical and artistic nudity, religious imagery and depictions of violence. The App Store age rating reflects this. Any individual work can be removed permanently with the Hide button.

Data security

All network requests use HTTPS. No credentials, tokens or personal data exist in the app, so there is nothing to encrypt at rest beyond what iOS already protects, and nothing that could be exposed by a breach of a server — because there is no server.

Data transfers

No personal data is transferred anywhere, internationally or otherwise. The museum APIs listed above are operated in the United States, Denmark, Japan and by the Wikimedia Foundation; contacting them is a download, not a transfer of your data.

Apple

If you have opted in to sharing analytics with Apple, or to Apple's own App Analytics for developers, Apple may provide the developer with aggregate, anonymised statistics such as install counts and crash reports. This is a service of the App Store rather than of this app, it contains nothing that identifies you, and it is governed by Apple's Privacy Policy. You can change it in Settings → Privacy & Security → Analytics & Improvements.

Changes to this policy

If the app ever begins collecting data — which is not planned — this policy will be updated before that version ships, and the change will be described here with a new date. Material changes will also be noted in the App Store release notes.
